==============================================================================
KHMER WORD SEGMENTATION  -  dictionary vs CRF
==============================================================================

[1] THE PROBLEM
    Khmer is written without spaces between words:
      បឹងកេតធ្លាប់បានក្លាយជាជើងឯកនៃពានរង្វាន់សម្តេចតេជោនេះ១លើកកាលពីឆ្នាំ២០១៩ ន
    That is 3 whitespace-separated chunks. Before anything else
    can happen - counting words, building features, training a model -
    the boundaries have to be found.

[2] THE TWO APPROACHES

    DICTIONARY (maximum matching)
      Walk left to right; at each position take the longest string present in a
      Khmer wordlist. Deterministic and fully inspectable - you can point at the
      dictionary and say why a cut was made. But it can only find words the
      dictionary already contains.

    CRF (conditional random field)
      A model trained on hand-segmented Khmer. For each gap between characters
      it predicts boundary or no-boundary from the surrounding characters.
      Handles unseen words, but you cannot inspect why it cut where it did.

[3] THE DICTIONARY
    source  : SIL NRSI khmerlbdict (MIT licence)
    entries : 34,398 unique words
    longest : 30 characters
    assembled from frequency wordlists, name lists and place lists;
    the files and licence are kept in data/dictionary/

[4] BOTH RUN ON OUR 400 SENTENCES
                     tokens  vocabulary  mean/sentence   seconds
    dictionary        14488        3143           36.2       0.1
    CRF               11615        3034           29.0       0.7

    dictionary tokens left unmatched : 2202  (15.2%)
    Those are positions where no dictionary word fit, so a single
    cluster was emitted. They are the visible cost of the dictionary
    approach - every one is a word the wordlist does not contain.

[6] WHICH WE USE, AND WHY
    We use the CRF (khmer-nltk) for the pipeline.

    The dictionary approach is easier to explain and to audit, and on a fixed
    vocabulary it is perfectly good. Our corpus is news text, which is full of
    proper nouns, place names, organisation names and numbers - exactly the
    material a fixed wordlist misses. Every unmatched position becomes a
    single-cluster fragment, and those fragments then become features, so the
    error propagates all the way to the classifier.

    The CRF was trained on hand-segmented Khmer and generalises to words it has
    not seen. We keep the dictionary implementation because it is the honest
    comparison: it shows what the CRF is buying us, and it is a fallback if the
    model is unavailable.

[7] LIMITATION
    Neither output is checked against a gold segmentation - we have no
    hand-segmented Khmer of our own. The comparison shows the two
    methods differ and by how much, not which is correct. Measuring
    that would need a segmented reference such as the khPOS corpus.
