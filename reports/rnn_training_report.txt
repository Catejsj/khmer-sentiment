    Why a recurrent network at all: the eight classical models read a
    fixed-length vector per sentence and cannot see word order. An RNN
    reads word by word and carries a hidden state, so order is part of
    the input. In Khmer that matters for negation - មិន placed before a
    positive word inverts the sentence.

[WHAT TO CONCLUDE]
    Both variants stop improving within a handful of epochs and then
    overfit - 408 training sentences cannot support a network with an
    embedding table this size. The classical models on averaged
    embeddings do better precisely because averaging is a much stronger
    constraint than letting a recurrent layer learn freely.

    That is the honest finding: on this dataset the RNN is included
    because the brief requires it and because it is the only model that
    sees word order, not because it wins. With a few thousand more
    annotated sentences the comparison would likely change.
